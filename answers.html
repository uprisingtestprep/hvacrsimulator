Chapter 2: Heat, Temperature and Pressure

Question 1
Correct answer: B.
Why the other choices are wrong: Heat is an amount of thermal energy, counted in BTU, and temperature shows how intense that heat is, read in degrees. Saying heat measures intensity and temperature measures total energy swaps the two definitions. The claim that they are the same quantity ignores that a pool and a bucket at one temperature hold very different amounts of heat. Describing temperature as the energy counted in BTU reverses the units.

A figure of 25 BTU treats melting as a one-degree sensible change, but the temperature does not change. The 970 BTU per pound figure belongs to boiling, not melting. Using 32 BTU per pound confuses the freezing point temperature with an amount of heat.
Question 3

Gauge and absolute readings differ by about 14.7 psi at sea level and agree only in a perfect vacuum. No 1.25 multiplier exists in the relationship.

Using half the drop halves the answer without reason, and leaving out the 1.08 factor drops the density and specific heat of air.
Question 5

The 24 ton answer miscalculates the product by doubling it.
Question 6
Correct answer: A.
Why the other choices are wrong: Boiling point depends on the pressure over the liquid, and atmospheric pressure falls from 14.7 psi at sea level to about 12.2 psi at 5,000 feet, so the saturation temperature falls to about 203 degrees Fahrenheit. Dissolved oxygen does not set the boiling point. Gravity changes only a trivial amount over that height. Cold air does not lower the boiling point of water.
Question 7
Correct answer: A.
Why the other choices are wrong: Absolute pressure equals 29.92 minus 25, or 4.92 inches of mercury, and 4.92 times 0.491 is about 2.4 psia. Multiplying the vacuum reading itself by 0.491 gives the amount of vacuum in psi, 12.3, and not the pressure that remains. Atmospheric pressure is 14.7 psia only with no vacuum. Halving atmospheric pressure is arbitrary and does not match the reading.
Question 8

A result of 0.20 is the latent share, the opposite quantity. A result of 0.25 divides the latent heat by the sensible heat, which is not a ratio of any standard use here. The value of 0.60 comes from a formula that does not exist.
Question 9

Why the other choices are wrong: With a fixed volume, absolute pressure is proportional to absolute temperature, so the pressure rises as the cylinder warms. A rigid container still shows a pressure change, so the claim that pressure stays the same is wrong. The gas cannot expand in a rigid cylinder, so density does not fall. The halving claim has no physical basis, and heat flows into the gas from the warmer surroundings.
Question 10
Correct answer: B.

The first description defines sensible heat instead. Radiation is a separate transfer method and is not the same as latent heat. A dry bulb thermometer reads sensible temperature, which is exactly what latent heat does not change.

Question 7

The metering device lowers pressure without adding heat, since enthalpy stays about the same.

Question 9

Question 10

An HFO is defined by a carbon double bond and by a very short atmospheric life, and R-22 has neither of these features.
Question 3
Correct answer: C.
Why the other choices are wrong: Superheat equals the actual suction line temperature minus the saturation temperature at suction pressure, or 49 minus 40, which is 9 degrees. The probe reading alone is the vapor temperature and does not show the amount of superheat.
Question 4

Adding the readings is not a meaningful operation here. The probe reading alone gives no comparison to saturation. A pressure in psig is not a temperature in degrees, so it cannot be read directly as one.
Question 5

Why the other choices are wrong: The components of a zeotropic blend boil at different rates, so drawing vapor takes mostly the lighter component and changes the mix left in the cylinder and in the system. Question 6

It does not corrode copper, so the claim about copper reactions is wrong.
Question 7

Lower toxicity with no flame propagation is class A1, as for R-134a.
Question 8

Question 9

Motor oil contains additives that contaminate a sealed system.
